# Validation

Do our numbers agree with published science? Each row uses the same years and the
same heights as the published result. Generated by `python -m lapse validate`.

| Published finding | Source | Our data | Our number | Match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Above 6 hPa (about 36 km and higher) the air cooled by 0.65 ± 0.11 K per decade, 2003 to 2012. | Pan and others, Journal of Climate, 2017 | AIRS (NASA Aqua satellite), 2003-01 to 2012-12 | -0.65 K per decade (range -1.13 to -0.25) | Yes. The ranges overlap. |
| Between 60 and 100 hPa (about 16 to 20 km) there was no significant trend, 2003 to 2012. | Pan and others, Journal of Climate, 2017 | AIRS (NASA Aqua satellite), 2003-01 to 2012-12 | +0.03 K per decade (range -0.32 to +0.37) | Yes. Ours is not proven either. |
| A warming trend in the lowest part of the atmosphere, 2002 to 2020. | NASA JPL, AIRS temperature trends, 2021 | AIRS (NASA Aqua satellite), 2002-09 to 2020-12 | +0.17 K per decade (range +0.08 to +0.25) | Yes. |
| A strong cooling trend in the upper stratosphere, 2002 to 2020. | NASA JPL, AIRS temperature trends, 2021 | AIRS (NASA Aqua satellite), 2002-09 to 2020-12 | -0.67 K per decade (range -0.92 to -0.40) | Yes. |
| The layer 25 to 50 km up cooled by 1 to 2 °C in total over 1986 to 2022. | Santer and others, PNAS, 2023 | MERRA-2 (NASA reanalysis), 1986-01 to 2022-12 | -0.65 K per decade (range -0.75 to -0.55), which is -2.4 °C in total | Same direction, different size. |

## How to read this

- Our layer value is a simple average of the pressure levels we have inside the layer.
  Published studies weight heights the way each satellite instrument sees them, so
  small differences in size are expected.
- The Santer comparison uses MERRA-2, a model corrected by measurements. Lapse's own
  jump check flags MERRA-2 above about 40 km, so treat that row with care.
- Trends: Theil-Sen slope. Test: Mann-Kendall with a correction for month-to-month memory.
